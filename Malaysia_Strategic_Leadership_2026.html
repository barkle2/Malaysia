Participation inside the Ministry

Condition 1 of 4

A strong message from the Ministry

We use AI to ease staff workloads
and tailor services to the public’s needs.

The Ministry’s message gives staff the backing to try building AI themselves.

Conditions for building AI in-house

Condition 2 of 4

AI skills across the workforce

Colleagues are the first testers.

Staff who build

Prototype a solution and improve it with feedback from the people doing the work.

Staff who use it

Try the tool on a real task, spot weak answers and explain what would make it useful.

Conditions for building AI in-house

Condition 3 of 4

Infrastructure and rules for AI development

Access to models

Fund approved AI model access, or host models on ministry infrastructure where needed.

Access to source material

Provide common sources such as statutes and judgements, with access appropriate to the data.

When the Ministry makes common sources available, staff can spend less time collecting the same material.

Conditions for building AI in-house

Condition 4 of 4

Recognition for staff who experiment

Many staff willingly spend their own time and money to improve the way we work.

Praise and encouragement can give staff enough reason to keep trying.

Conditions for building AI in-house

Why it’s worth persisting

A tool may fail
Even a tool that works may go unused. Generative AI can give wrong answers.
Civil servants can build
We know our fields and have the job security to try something new.
“Ace” colleagues may push back
People who excel at the current way of working can be reluctant to change it.
Learning from trials

How does an organisation adapt to change?

Change happens when people try something new.
